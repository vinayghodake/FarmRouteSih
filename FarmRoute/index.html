STATUTORY LEGAL REMEDIES UNDER INDIAN LAW

Anti-Fraud & Legal Protection Shield

Know your legal rights under the Maharashtra APMC Act, Negotiable Instruments Act (Section 138), and Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS / IPC) against defaulting traders, bounced cheques, and delayed payments.

⚖️ Sec 138 NI Act Enforced 📜 APMC Rule 32 Enforced
⚖️ Your Legal Weapons Against Trader Fraud
1. Maharashtra APMC Act, 1963 (Section 32 & 37) — Mandatory Same-Day Payment

Traders and commission agents (आडते) are legally bound to pay farmers on the same day of auction or within 24 hours. Non-payment gives the APMC Secretary the legal power to seize the trader's mandatory bank guarantee deposit and cancel their trading license immediately.

2. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (Section 138) — Bounced Cheque Criminal Case

If a buyer or trader gives you a cheque that bounces due to insufficient funds, it is a cognizable criminal offence. You have the legal right to send a formal 15-day statutory Demand Notice. Failure to pay within 15 days makes the buyer liable for up to 2 years imprisonment plus a fine equal to double the cheque amount.

3. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (Sec 316 & 318) / IPC 406 & 420 — Criminal Breach of Trust

If a buyer takes custody of your crop under false promises or deceitful intention and absconds, an immediate FIR can be lodged under Section 316 (Criminal Breach of Trust) and Section 318 (Cheating) with the Police Station / Cyber Crime Cell.

4. National Agriculture Market (e-NAM) & APMC Dispute Resolution Committee

Every APMC has a statutory Dispute Sub-Committee under the District Deputy Registrar (DDR) to resolve payment disputes within 7 days, with power of attachment of the buyer's property.
